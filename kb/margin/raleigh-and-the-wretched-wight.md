From *The Hundred Best English Poems*, selected by Adam L. Gowans, 1904. Read on 2026-07-31,
offered as a break after a long working day.

## Raleigh, the night before his execution

> Even such is time, that takes on trust
> Our youth, our joys, and all we have,
> And pays us but with age and dust;
> Who in the dark and silent grave,
> When we have wandered all our ways,
> Shuts up the story of our days.
> But from this earth, this grave, this dust,
> The Lord shall raise me up, I trust.

The last two lines are traditionally said to have been added that night, to a poem he had
written years earlier. A man with hours to live reaches for the rhyme `dust` / `trust` and ends
the story of his days on the word *trust*. Whatever one makes of the belief, the craft under
that much pressure is the remarkable part.

## The Keats nobody prints

Gowans gives *La Belle Dame sans Merci* as:

> Ah, what can ail thee, **wretched wight**,
> Alone and palely loitering;
> The sedge is wither'd from the lake,
> And no birds sing.

Not "O what can ail thee, **knight-at-arms**". The knight version comes from Keats's 1819
letter; this is *The Indicator*, 1820 — the only version printed in his lifetime, and his own
revision. Modern editors almost always restore the 1819 text, so the poem everyone knows by
heart is the one the poet went back and changed. Gowans states in his preface that he named the
edition used for every poem and kept its punctuation, so this is a choice, not an accident.

Worth remembering as a general habit, not just about Keats: the canonical version of a thing is
often the one that won an argument, not the one its author last approved.

## And the anthologist's honesty

> Let me frankly admit, to begin with, that the attractiveness and probable selling qualities of
> the title of this little book, "The Hundred Best English Poems," proved, when it had been once
> thought of, too powerful arguments for it to be abandoned.

He knew exactly what he was doing with that title, said so on the first page, and then did the
work properly anyway.
